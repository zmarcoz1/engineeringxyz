Services

Mechanical Engineering Services for Manufacturers and Hardware Teams

Every service below is scoped as a defined engineering deliverable — not an open-ended retainer. Most engagements begin with a small first scope, then grow only if the work justifies it.

01

Machine Prototypes and Production Equipment

Typical situation: A machine or equipment concept exists — a sketch, a rough CAD model, a proof-of-principle — but nobody has taken it to a design that can be built, quoted, and commissioned.

Typical problems: Unclear mechanical architecture, unresolved motion and actuation choices, no drawings, incomplete vendor handoff, or a prototype that works once but cannot survive production use.

Benefit: A prototype or equipment design that is actually buildable — with architecture decisions made deliberately, not discovered during the build.

Discuss a prototype Related project →

Possible Deliverables

  • Concept layouts and mechanical architecture
  • 3D CAD models
  • Engineering drawings and vendor-ready packages
  • Build-readiness review
  • Equipment layouts and commissioning support

02

Fixture, Workholding, and Poka-Yoke Design

Typical situation: Operators are positioning parts by feel, a process depends on skill instead of tooling, or an assembly step keeps producing variation nobody can trace.

Typical problems: Inconsistent positioning, slow loading and unloading, operator-to-operator variation, mis-loads that reach downstream stations, and workholding that fights the part.

Benefit: Repeatable positioning, faster cycle times, and processes that no longer depend on individual operator technique.

Discuss a fixture Related project →

Possible Deliverables

  • Fixture concepts and workholding systems
  • Poka-yoke (mistake-proofing) features
  • Operator-flow improvements
  • Tolerance and interface review
  • 3D CAD and fabrication drawings

03

Inspection and Test Systems

Typical situation: Inspection or test is a bottleneck: parts queue up, results vary by operator, or measurements depend on how the part happens to be staged.

Typical problems: Inspection bottlenecks, non-repeatable part presentation, manual staging, gauge-access problems, and test setups that cannot keep pace with production.

Benefit: Consistent, repeatable measurements and inspection throughput that keeps up with the line.

Discuss an inspection system Related project →

Possible Deliverables

  • Inspection positioning systems
  • Test-station mechanical design
  • Staging and part-presentation fixtures
  • Integration with existing gauges and optics
  • Engineering documentation

04

Mechanical Design and Engineering Drawings

Typical situation: Designs are stuck between concept and release: models exist but drawings do not, tolerances were never analyzed, or a vendor returned a quote full of questions.

Typical problems: Unfinished drawings, undocumented design intent, tolerance stack-ups nobody checked, interfaces that do not quite fit, and drawing backlogs blocking releases.

Benefit: Documentation that vendors and machine shops can quote and build from without a chain of clarification emails.

Discuss a design package Related project →

Possible Deliverables

  • 3D CAD modeling
  • Engineering drawings (GD&T as appropriate)
  • Tolerance and interface review
  • Design-for-assembly review
  • Drawing-release support

05

Manufacturing and Production Engineering Support

Typical situation: A production step needs dedicated equipment that does not exist off the shelf — or existing equipment needs modification the OEM will not support.

Typical problems: Manual steps that limit throughput, equipment concepts too expensive as first drafted, and machines specified without regard for maintenance access or operator safety.

Benefit: Purpose-built equipment matched to the real constraints of your line — footprint, operators, utilities, and maintenance.

Discuss production equipment Related project →

Possible Deliverables

  • Equipment layouts
  • Mechanical architecture and simplification studies
  • 3D CAD and engineering drawings
  • Design-for-assembly review
  • Vendor-ready build packages

06

Project Recovery and Engineering Capacity

Typical situation: Your mechanical backlog exceeds your team, a hire is months away, or a project inherited from a departed engineer or a vendor has stalled mid-stream.

Typical problems: Backlogged mechanical work, drawing-release workload, vendor coordination overhead, half-finished designs with missing context, and deadlines that will not move.

Benefit: Qualified mechanical engineering capacity on a project basis — without a hiring cycle, and without committing to headcount.

Possible Deliverables

  • Design review and troubleshooting
  • Project recovery assessments
  • Overflow design and drawing work
  • Vendor coordination
  • Engineering documentation and handoff packages
