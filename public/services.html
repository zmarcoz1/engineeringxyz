Who Does What: Machine Electrical Scope

In California, machine electrical work splits across three parties. Naming that split before a project starts is the difference between a clean build and an argument about whose drawing was wrong.

Scope Who Why
Control architecture, schematics, I/O, PLC and HMI programming, component specification, safety-circuit design EngineeringXYZ Design and programming for a manufacturer's own production equipment
Enclosure build, component mounting, internal panel wiring, short-circuit current rating, nameplate and markings A UL 508A industrial control panel shop, engaged by you Panel fabrication and labelling is a listed-shop function
Facility branch circuit, overcurrent protection, disconnect, conduit and raceway, field conductors, grounding and bonding to the premises, permits, and final connection A licensed C-10 electrical contractor, engaged directly by you Fixed electrical installation in a building requires a California contractor licence
Whether the completed machine requires listing or an NRTL field evaluation You, with your local authority having jurisdiction A UL 508A panel label certifies the panel — not the machine it controls

EngineeringXYZ provides the documentation each of these parties needs and answers their questions during the build. It does not contract for, supervise, or perform installation work, and does not direct the means and methods of licensed trades.

Controls, Installation, and Performance Boundaries

Controls support may include control architecture, PLC/HMI software, interface definitions, and design documentation within the agreed industrial scope. Panel fabrication, field wiring, facility connections, permits, inspections, and electrical installation are performed and accepted by the client's qualified panel shop and appropriately licensed electrical contractor.

Performance figures are project-specific objectives, not warranties, unless expressly stated as written acceptance criteria in a signed agreement. The client remains responsible for final site validation, production release, operation, training, maintenance, and later modifications, subject to the responsibility matrix in the agreement.
